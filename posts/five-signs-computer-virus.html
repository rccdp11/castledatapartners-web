5 Signs Your Computer Has a Virus (And What To Do About It)

Rinaldo Castillo May 20, 2026 5 min read

Your computer has been acting a little strange lately. Maybe it's slower than usual, or you're seeing pop-ups you didn't ask for. Before you panic — or worse, ignore it — here are five clear signs that your computer may be infected, and exactly what to do next.

1. Your Computer Is Suddenly Slow

One of the most common signs of a virus is a dramatic slowdown. If your computer used to start up in 30 seconds and now takes three minutes, something is using your processor and memory in the background — and malware is a likely culprit.

What to do: Open Task Manager (Windows) or Activity Monitor (Mac) and look for unfamiliar processes using a lot of CPU or memory. Don't recognize something? That's worth investigating.

2. Pop-Ups Keep Appearing

If you're getting pop-up ads even when your browser is closed, or you're seeing warnings claiming your computer is infected and urging you to call a number — that's a red flag. Legitimate companies do not contact you this way.

What to do: Do not click anything in those pop-ups. Don't call any numbers they display. Close them with Task Manager if needed, and run a malware scan.

3. Your Browser Has Changed on Its Own

If your homepage changed, you have a new search engine you didn't install, or there are toolbars and extensions you don't recognize — your browser has likely been hijacked by adware or a browser hijacker.

What to do: Go into your browser settings, remove unfamiliar extensions, and reset your homepage and default search engine. Then run a full scan.

4. You're Getting Unexplained Crashes or Errors

Frequent blue screens (Windows) or application crashes that didn't used to happen can signal corrupted system files — sometimes caused by malware that interferes with core operating system components.

What to do: Note when the crashes happen and what you were doing. Run Windows Defender or your preferred antivirus in full-scan mode.

5. Friends Are Getting Strange Messages From You

If someone tells you they received a weird email or social media message from your account that you didn't send — your account (or device) has likely been compromised. This is a sign of credential-stealing malware.

What to do: Immediately change your passwords from a different, clean device. Enable two-factor authentication on all your accounts. Then clean your infected machine.

When in doubt, don't try to fix it yourself. The wrong move can make things worse — or cause you to lose important files.

What To Do If You Think You're Infected

  • Disconnect from the internet to stop the malware from communicating outward
  • Run a full scan with Windows Defender, Malwarebytes, or another reputable tool
  • Back up your important files to an external drive (after scanning it)
  • If the infection is severe, a full OS reinstall may be the cleanest fix
  • Call a professional if you're not comfortable doing this yourself
